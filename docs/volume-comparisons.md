# Volume comparison objects

For the share card that translates session volume into something a person can picture. Draft, meant to be edited.

Weights are in pounds, rounded to something defensible rather than precise. The card picks the heaviest entry at or below the session volume, so the line always reads as an achievement rather than a shortfall.

## Rules

Below 400 lb, skip the comparison card entirely rather than reaching for something tiny. A first session with one light set does not need to be told it moved a bag of dog food.

Above 80,000 lb, keep the semi truck and do not multiply ("that is 3 semi trucks"). Multiples stop being picturable, which is the whole point.

Store the weights in pounds and convert for kg users at display time, so one table serves both. Round the converted figure rather than showing the object at a suspiciously precise weight.

## Why these

Every entry is something a person can hold a mental image of at actual size. Skipped deliberately: buildings, aircraft, anything measured in tons rather than pounds, and anything a beginner would have to look up. The list leans on animals and vehicles because those are the two categories where almost everyone has a sense of scale.

The T. rex is the one entry that is not from everyday life, and it earns its place because it is the most shareable line on the list.

## Open question

Volume totals depend on how much a person trains, not how strong they are. A high-rep session moves more total weight than a heavy low-rep one. The card will sometimes tell a stronger lifter they moved less. Worth watching once real users see it, and a reason not to lean too hard on this number as a measure of anything.
